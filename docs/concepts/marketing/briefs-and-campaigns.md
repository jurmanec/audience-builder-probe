# Briefs and Campaigns

## Campaign

A **campaign** is a coordinated marketing effort aimed at a defined audience to achieve a specific business objective (for example, driving fiber upgrades, reducing churn, or promoting a new product).

It typically includes an objective, target audience, message or offer, channels, timing, and success metrics. In large telecom marketing, campaigns can be one-time initiatives or always-on lifecycle programs. The household data and attribute catalog are used to build and size the audiences that campaigns target.

## Brief (campaign brief)

A **brief** is the structured document that captures the essential decisions for a campaign so that execution teams can act on them.

It normally includes sections such as:
- Objective
- Target audience
- Key message
- Offer or promo mechanics
- Channels
- KPIs / success metrics
- Constraints and mandatories

In the P6 project specifically, a brief is a short, grounded record produced only after an audience has been approved. It is filled from the approved audience plus the past-campaign library; it is not open-ended creative writing. Its purpose is to turn a validated segment into a clear handoff artifact.

## How they relate to template intents and predicates

- A **template intent** is a known campaign type whose audience logic has already been turned into fixed predicates.
- Those **predicates** select the audience from the household table.
- Once the audience is approved, a **brief** can be generated for that campaign, grounded in the audience and prior campaign examples.

In short: predicates define who the campaign reaches; the brief records what the campaign is trying to do and how.
